# Chapter illustration brief

This brief defines the thematic illustration set for *Better Code and Gardens*.
It covers every chapter and adds section illustrations only where the subject or
reading rhythm changes enough to justify one. It is a production brief, not an
asset manifest: filenames, SVG construction, responsive markup, and generator
integration belong to the next stage.

## Set direction

The set should look like one illuminated gardening manuscript made for a book
about a working system. Each image should explain a relationship in the text.
Use recognizable objects, paths, boundaries, and transformations rather than a
generic spray of leaves. Ornamental borders may include vines, roots, seedpods,
small tools, insects, and inhabited initials, but ornament must support the
diagrammatic idea.

Vary the scenes and viewpoints. Alternate broad landscapes, cutaways, maps,
still lifes, and close marginal studies so the recurring garden metaphor does
not become a repeated garden-bed picture. Fine line work, gilded-manuscript
geometry, decorated initials, and botanical marginalia should unify the set.
The two recurring motifs below are occasional rewards for attentive readers:

- A distant wizard tower with a beacon may appear where supervision, routing,
  or a signal crossing distance is already part of the scene.
- Hanging gardens may appear where layered memory, hosted systems, or bounded
  resources make terraces a natural metaphor.

Do not put either motif in every image, and never enlarge either into a mascot
or foreground joke. **Anchoring the color scheme is the next step's job.** The
production stage should choose how the manuscript treatment relates to the
current light and dark schemes; this brief intentionally specifies no exact
colors or fixed palette.

Chapter openers should normally sit after the chapter heading and provenance,
before the first paragraph. Section images should follow the named section
heading, near the first conceptual turn, with enough surrounding prose that
they do not form an uninterrupted gallery. The suggested ratios are compositional
guidance, not hard crops. Meaningful images need concise alternative text that
states the depicted relationship. Pure flourishes inside an otherwise meaningful
figure should be hidden from assistive technology. Captions should add the
interpretive link rather than repeat the alternative text.

## Proposed images

### 1. The garden through its metamorphoses

- **Target anchor:** `ch1-chapter-1-philosophy-history-and-metamorphosis`
- **Central theme:** The garden is one continuing practice rebuilt into several
  forms as each earlier form reaches its limit.
- **Visual brief:** Show one old garden path passing through four successive
  enclosures: a solitary shepherd's plot, a protected glasshouse, a larger
  supervised nursery, and a distributed garden whose channels meet at a central
  ledger. The ground and one persistent root system continue through all four,
  making continuity and rebuilding legible. A small beacon tower may be visible
  on the horizon of the supervised stage, secondary to the changing gardens.
- **Illuminated treatment:** Use a four-lobed border like successive manuscript
  compartments, joined by the same root and climbing vine. Let tools from one
  compartment become architectural ornaments in the next.
- **Accessibility and pacing:** Meaningful chapter opener; use a broad 3:2 or
  16:10 composition. Alternative text should name the four transformations and
  the continuous root. Keep detail away from the lower text-side edge so the
  image remains readable when reduced on phones.

### 2. A market for the next plot of work

- **Target anchor:** `ch1-15-the-next-metamorphosis-the-bidding-market`
- **Central theme:** The proposed market replaces a speed-only claim race with
  selection based on fit, cost, and learned reputation.
- **Visual brief:** Depict gardeners at the edge of several unlike plots offering
  labeled seed tokens into a balanced market scale. The selected gardener's tool
  and seed match the needs of one plot, while a faded footrace path behind the
  market shows the older first-to-arrive rule without making it the focal point.
- **Illuminated treatment:** Frame the scale with curling pea vines and tiny
  ledger marks in the border. Use heraldic tool shapes to distinguish bidders,
  not faces or modern auction imagery.
- **Accessibility and pacing:** A horizontal 5:2 section illustration can break
  the long historical chapter before its forward-looking final movement.
  Alternative text should contrast fastest arrival with best fit. Do not rely
  on tiny written labels to convey the distinction.

### 3. The machinery beneath one shared garden

- **Target anchor:** `ch2-chapter-2-architecture-and-operation`
- **Central theme:** Many independent workers coordinate through shared state,
  isolated workspaces, and explicit operating rules.
- **Visual brief:** Use a cutaway of a walled garden. Above ground, gardeners
  tend separate beds; below ground, channels converge on a bound journal at the
  center. Separate potting sheds make worktree isolation visible, while gates
  and paths show that coordination does not require everyone to share a room.
- **Illuminated treatment:** Treat the cutaway as a medieval estate diagram with
  an ornate compass rose made from a flower. Roots can double as routing lines,
  but each route must remain traceable.
- **Accessibility and pacing:** Meaningful 3:2 chapter opener. Its caption should
  connect separate beds to isolated worktrees and the central book to the
  journal. Preserve large silhouettes at narrow widths; fine root detail is
  supplemental.

### 4. One journal, three duties

- **Target anchor:** `ch2-21-the-journal-as-job-board-and-message-bus`
- **Central theme:** The journal is simultaneously transcript, job board, and
  message bus, with Git pushes providing the common serialization point.
- **Visual brief:** Show an open triptych book. One leaf records dated events,
  one bears three trays labeled by state through placement and symbols, and one
  carries sealed messages along branching stems. All three leaves fasten to one
  central clasp, making the single coordination surface unmistakable.
- **Illuminated treatment:** Build the triptych from decorated manuscript pages
  whose vines cross the gutters and meet at the clasp. Small seed, sprout, and
  bloom marks can denote pending, active, and completed work without depending
  on text.
- **Accessibility and pacing:** Use a wide 2:1 section figure after the opening
  explanation. Alternative text should explicitly name transcript, board, bus,
  and their shared journal. A short caption may introduce the clasp as the
  accepted push.

### 5. A deliberate deploy across the fleet

- **Target anchor:** `ch2-26-the-deliberate-deploy`
- **Central theme:** A tested version moves intentionally from development to
  canary followers and only then to the leader, while deployed roots remain
  stable.
- **Visual brief:** Depict a graft carried from a nursery bench to a row of small
  orchard trees. It is tried on two outer trees before reaching the central old
  tree. Tags on the trunks show that each tree holds one settled graft at a time;
  loose cuttings remain confined to the nursery.
- **Illuminated treatment:** Use a processional border of pruning knives, grafting
  tape, and leaves. A distant tower beacon may echo the upgrade-ready signal, no
  larger than a thumbprint in the full composition.
- **Accessibility and pacing:** Horizontal 5:2 works as a process sequence.
  Alternative text should state the order: development, canaries, leader. Avoid
  animation; if integration later adds progressive reveal, reduced-motion users
  must receive the complete static scene.

### 6. Speaking through the garden gate

- **Target anchor:** `ch3-chapter-3-using-the-garden`
- **Central theme:** The reader speaks to one liaison, who translates intent into
  durable work for a fleet and returns the fleet's questions.
- **Visual brief:** Place a reader at an open garden gate speaking with a steward.
  Beyond the gate, labeled forms become seed packets carried to distant workers,
  and reply ribbons return to a sheltered message box. The steward remains at
  the threshold rather than doing the field work.
- **Illuminated treatment:** Make the gate an architectural initial surrounded by
  ivy, with small speech scrolls becoming paths. The composition should feel
  welcoming and practical, not courtly.
- **Accessibility and pacing:** A portrait-leaning 4:3 opener suits the human
  entry point. Alternative text should identify the liaison as relay, not worker.
  Keep all essential action in the center so a wider desktop crop and full-width
  mobile presentation tell the same story.

### 7. Muster turns an overgrown inbox into decisions

- **Target anchor:** `ch3-33-muster-working-the-maintainer-inbox`
- **Central theme:** Muster compacts, classifies, and disposes of accumulated
  messages in three deliberate passes.
- **Visual brief:** Show an overgrown potting table becoming orderly from left to
  right: a tangled pile of message leaves is pressed into concise bundles, sorted
  into a few baskets, then presented one basket at a time to a seated gardener.
  Nothing should suggest that the system decides all dispositions unaided.
- **Illuminated treatment:** Use three linked miniatures under one arched vine,
  with pruning, basket, and pointing-hand ornaments marking the passes.
- **Accessibility and pacing:** A wide 3:1 strip is appropriate midway through
  this practical chapter. Alternative text should name the three passes in
  order. The stages must remain distinguishable without color.

### 8. A garden instance as a living enclosure

- **Target anchor:** `ch4-chapter-4-creating-your-own-instance`
- **Central theme:** Creating an instance means establishing a distinct,
  credentialed, supervised environment that can join a larger shared garden.
- **Visual brief:** Show a new walled plot being laid out from an empty site: a
  boundary, a named gate, a tool chest with three locks, worker beds, and a path
  joining a larger network beyond the wall. The scene should read as provisioning
  infrastructure, not merely planting a seed.
- **Illuminated treatment:** Present the plan as an illuminated foundation chart,
  with a decorated plumb line and compass among modest botanical marginalia.
- **Accessibility and pacing:** Meaningful 3:2 opener. Alternative text should
  name enclosure, unique identity, credentials, workers, and shared connection.
  Leave an uncluttered edge for the chapter's long introductory copy.

### 9. The container boundary

- **Target anchor:** `ch4-42-the-container-model`
- **Central theme:** The long-lived container separates the autonomous bot fleet
  from the human host and its credentials while still providing a complete
  operating environment.
- **Visual brief:** Cut away a glass cloche or conservatory. Inside are the worker
  beds, clockwork service wheels, and a bot's locked tool cabinet. Outside sit
  the human's house keys and private tools, visibly unreachable across the glass.
  A single guarded doorway represents the launcher and container check.
- **Illuminated treatment:** Use the cloche as a circular medallion ringed by
  thornless protective vines and small lock-and-key marginal symbols.
- **Accessibility and pacing:** Near-square 1:1 or 4:3 section figure. Alternative
  text must state what is inside, what stays outside, and why the boundary exists.
  Do not use color alone to distinguish host and container.

### 10. The turnkey host arrives empty of secrets

- **Target anchor:** `ch4-46-the-turnkey-path-a-disposable-aws-host`
- **Central theme:** A disposable cloud host can arrive prebuilt but receives
  credentials only through the human-controlled first-entry process.
- **Visual brief:** Depict a compact floating garden terrace descending from a
  cloud by winch, already fitted with beds and a conservatory but with three
  empty key niches. A human on the ground supplies keys only after it lands.
  The hanging-garden image should arise from the hosted, replaceable nature of
  the instance, not serve as spectacle.
- **Illuminated treatment:** Border the miniature with rain, cloud, and root
  ornaments that terminate cleanly at the page edge. Keep the terrace structurally
  plausible within the manuscript fantasy.
- **Accessibility and pacing:** Wide 16:9 section image before the resource and
  launch details. Alternative text should contrast prebuilt infrastructure with
  absent secrets. Keep cloud-provider branding out of the picture; the prose and
  caption can name the platform.

### 11. Roles as the garden's field guilds

- **Target anchor:** `ch5-chapter-5-roles-reference`
- **Central theme:** A role is a focused operating brief that gives a generic
  gardener a temporary posture and the relevant playbooks.
- **Visual brief:** Arrange one central gardener before a semicircle of tool
  alcoves: building, tending, judging, researching, operating, and repairing.
  The gardener wears no fixed uniform, but takes one emblem and one slim book
  from an alcove for the work at hand. This distinguishes role from worker kind.
- **Illuminated treatment:** Use a guild-page layout with six historiated niches,
  each entwined by a different plant family while sharing the same root border.
- **Accessibility and pacing:** Broad 3:2 chapter opener. Alternative text should
  explain one worker adopting different role briefs. The emblems must be bold
  enough to survive reduction; detailed catalog entries below need no additional
  illustration simply to fill space.

### 12. The panel as a many-eyed arbor

- **Target anchor:** `ch5-54-judicial-and-panel-adjacent-roles`
- **Central theme:** Specialized seats inspect one artifact from different
  angles, while deterministic machinery gathers their findings and controls the
  review loop.
- **Visual brief:** Place a manuscript folio on a rotating stand beneath an arbor.
  Several small observers examine its edges, joinery, language, locks, and roots
  through different lenses. Their ribbons converge into one sorting device,
  rather than into a crowned judge, to show that aggregation and control flow are
  scripted.
- **Illuminated treatment:** Give each lens a distinct botanical surround and
  let the arbor form a restrained courtroom arch. Avoid gavels, wigs, or a single
  dominant authority figure.
- **Accessibility and pacing:** A 2:1 section figure introduces the longest
  conceptual shift in the role catalog. Alternative text should say that many
  specialist reviews converge through a deterministic loop. Decorative seat
  details may be omitted in a small responsive variant.

### 13. Skills as a cabinet of reusable cultivation methods

- **Target anchor:** `ch6-chapter-6-skills-reference`
- **Central theme:** Skills are reusable, just-in-time playbooks, separate from
  the roles that choose them and from the scripts that mechanize them.
- **Visual brief:** Show an apothecary-like seed cabinet whose drawers contain
  slim procedure folios, tools, and measured packets. A gardener holding a role
  emblem opens only the few drawers needed for one task. Below the cabinet, small
  gears turn for the procedures that have deterministic implementations.
- **Illuminated treatment:** Treat the cabinet as an ornate architectural border
  with botanical drawer labels and tiny inhabited initials. The object should
  remain a working cabinet, not dissolve into abstract ornament.
- **Accessibility and pacing:** A 3:2 opener is the only general illustration
  needed for the catalog. Alternative text should distinguish selecting a
  playbook from executing its scripted mechanism. Favor strong grouped shapes
  over 98 literal drawers.

### 14. The guarded edge of the garden

- **Target anchor:** `ch6-610-security-and-trust-surfaces`
- **Central theme:** Material and references crossing into or out of the garden
  pass through explicit trust checks before they reach an agent or a public
  surface.
- **Visual brief:** Show a garden wall with two controlled openings. At one, an
  unfamiliar scroll is inspected under a glass before entering; at the other, a
  messenger checks that a destination tag is complete before a note leaves.
  Beyond the wall, paths remain deliberately indistinct.
- **Illuminated treatment:** Use bramble, gate, and warding-knot motifs around a
  clear central threshold. The checks should look procedural rather than fearful.
- **Accessibility and pacing:** Horizontal 5:2 section figure, providing a pause
  deep in the long skills catalog. Alternative text should identify inbound
  content classification and outbound reference validation. Do not portray
  foreign content itself as inherently malicious.

### 15. Work follows named paths

- **Target anchor:** `ch7-chapter-7-procedures-and-workflows`
- **Central theme:** The garden turns recurring work into explicit procedures so
  progress survives changing workers and interrupted sessions.
- **Visual brief:** Use an overhead garden map with several clearly different
  routes: a loop through inspection beds, a branching sequence of plots, a path
  waiting at a closed gate, and a river crossing. Signposts and terrain make the
  route choices legible without requiring labels.
- **Illuminated treatment:** Render it as a manuscript itinerary map bordered by
  vines whose knots echo decision points. Tiny gardeners may occupy the routes,
  but the routes themselves are the subject.
- **Accessibility and pacing:** A wide 16:10 opener. Alternative text should say
  that different procedures suit review loops, multi-part work, delayed triggers,
  and identity crossing. This overview reduces the need for an image at every
  workflow subsection.

### 16. The gauntlet tends one artifact through repeated review

- **Target anchor:** `ch7-72-the-gauntlet-end-to-end`
- **Central theme:** One draft pull request passes through viability, cleaning,
  specialist review, fixes, and a final readiness transition.
- **Visual brief:** Follow one potted espalier along a curved sequence of stations:
  root inspection, pruning, a many-lensed arbor, repair and regrowth, then an open
  display gate. If review finds a problem, the path visibly loops from the arbor
  back to the repair bench while the same pot continues throughout.
- **Illuminated treatment:** Use a continuous vine as both process arrow and
  decorated border. Repeated leaves can mark rounds without implying a fixed
  number of them.
- **Accessibility and pacing:** A long 3:1 figure can replace the text's abstract
  line diagram as the visual rest point, without replacing its technical detail.
  Alternative text should name the stages and the review-to-fix loop. Ensure the
  loop remains readable in grayscale and at mobile width.

### 17. Orchestration writes the whole sequence down

- **Target anchor:** `ch7-74-orchestration`
- **Central theme:** Multi-part work is made durable by recording children,
  order, failure policy, and budget before execution begins.
- **Visual brief:** Show a head gardener laying seed packets into a ruled planting
  plan. Some furrows run serially and one pair runs in parallel; stop and continue
  symbols stand at a damaged plot. A measured water reservoir feeds the whole
  plan, making a bounded shared budget visible.
- **Illuminated treatment:** Let the ruled plan resemble a decorated calendar
  page, with roots following the sequence lines and knots marking gates.
- **Accessibility and pacing:** Landscape 2:1 section illustration. Alternative
  text should mention recorded order, serial and parallel children, failure
  policy, and shared budget. Keep the plan's meaning pictorial; tiny written job
  names would not be accessible.

### 18. The ferry crosses an identity boundary

- **Target anchor:** `ch7-76-the-ferry`
- **Central theme:** Approved work crosses from the bot-owned fork to upstream
  only through a separate, human-authorized process using the maintainer's
  identity.
- **Visual brief:** Depict a ferryman carrying one sealed graft across a narrow
  river from a bot-tended bank to an upstream orchard. A human on the departure
  bank hands over a signet only for the crossing; ordinary garden paths stop at
  the water. The graft remains sealed and singular throughout.
- **Illuminated treatment:** Use river reeds, rope knots, and a small decorated
  boat in a low manuscript miniature. Keep the signet visible but not grandiose.
- **Accessibility and pacing:** Wide 5:2 section figure placed before the
  authorization details. Alternative text should name the bot side, upstream
  side, human authorization, and identity change. The river boundary must remain
  evident without relying on opposing colors.

### 19. The garden as a set of feedback loops

- **Target anchor:** `ch8-chapter-8-cybernetics-and-budgeting`
- **Central theme:** Spending and capacity are regulated by sensors, setpoints,
  controllers, and actuators that observe their own effects.
- **Visual brief:** Show an irrigation garden where gauges measure reservoirs,
  float valves compare levels with marked setpoints, gates change the flow, and
  the resulting soil moisture returns through roots to the gauges. Several loops
  run at visibly different scales without collapsing into one wheel.
- **Illuminated treatment:** Combine precise instrument drawing with an ornate
  circular vine border. Repeated leaf-and-valve forms can unify the loops while
  distinct paths prevent visual ambiguity.
- **Accessibility and pacing:** Meaningful 3:2 opener. Alternative text should
  enumerate sensor, setpoint, controller, actuator, and feedback. A caption may
  note that budget is one regulated quantity among several.

### 20. Cybernetics begins with the loop, not the ledger

- **Target anchor:** `ch8-81-why-cybernetics`
- **Central theme:** The key question is how measurement changes behavior and
  returns as a new measurement, including failure and human control points.
- **Visual brief:** Use one close study of a cistern, gauge, gardener-adjusted
  valve, irrigation bed, and return channel. Show a stuck float and an overflow
  notch as small secondary details, making sensor failure and saturation concrete.
- **Illuminated treatment:** Frame the loop inside a roundel with water plants and
  calibrated tick marks worked into the ornament.
- **Accessibility and pacing:** Near-square 1:1 image just after the five audit
  questions. Alternative text should trace the causal loop in order and mention
  the human-adjusted control. It should not duplicate the broader fleet scene of
  the chapter opener.

### 21. A bounded pie for an orchestration

- **Target anchor:** `ch8-86-per-orchestration-budgets-a-bounded-pie`
- **Central theme:** Serial child jobs draw from one finite campaign budget, and
  incomplete metering or exhaustion stops further promotion.
- **Visual brief:** Depict a measured round garden divided into planned beds fed
  from one finite seed bowl. A gardener fills beds in sequence; later gates stay
  closed when the bowl is empty or when the measuring scoop is missing. The
  finite quantity and admission decision must be immediately visible.
- **Illuminated treatment:** Echo a medieval calendar wheel, with seed heads and
  measuring tools in the border. Avoid a literal dessert pie, which would turn
  the book's own metaphor into a joke.
- **Accessibility and pacing:** Compact 4:3 section image before the mechanics.
  Alternative text should explain finite shared budget, serial admission, and
  fail-closed behavior when measurement is incomplete.

### 22. A library grown for retrieval

- **Target anchor:** `ch9-chapter-9-the-library-and-how-it-spends-context`
- **Central theme:** The library is cultivated so a worker can retrieve the
  smallest useful portion of accumulated knowledge within a limited context.
- **Visual brief:** Show a terraced hanging library garden whose shelves and beds
  descend from broad topics to source sections. A reader at ground level draws
  one small basket down by pulley instead of climbing through or harvesting the
  entire structure. The terraces make selective loading visually explicit.
- **Illuminated treatment:** Integrate book spines, trailing plants, pulleys, and
  labeled tabs into a tall manuscript architecture. The hanging gardens motif is
  appropriate here because the knowledge is layered, indexed, and selectively
  lowered to the reader.
- **Accessibility and pacing:** A portrait 4:5 chapter opener can occupy the
  margin on wide screens and become full-width on narrow screens. Alternative
  text should describe layered indexes and retrieval of one small relevant
  portion. Essential relationships must survive a landscape crop if the
  integration chooses one asset across breakpoints.

### 23. Three indexes around the content itself

- **Target anchor:** `ch9-92-what-it-looks-like-on-disk`
- **Central theme:** Source, topic, and keyword indexes provide different routes
  to section-sized content while preserving provenance and maintenance signals.
- **Visual brief:** Depict a central bed of bound folios reached by three paths:
  an archival path from source markers, a thematic path through named plots, and
  a quick stepping-stone path of keywords. Small pruning tags mark stale,
  superseded, and contradictory growth without removing it from view.
- **Illuminated treatment:** Use a clover-like three-way plan with a decorated
  central folio and distinct botanical path edging. This should feel like a map,
  not a file-tree screenshot.
- **Accessibility and pacing:** Square or 4:3 section figure placed after the
  directory sketch. Alternative text should name all three indexing axes and
  the central section content. A caption can connect pruning tags to maintenance
  metadata.

### 24. The context window is a reading basket

- **Target anchor:** `ch9-96-why-it-is-shaped-this-way-the-context-economy`
- **Central theme:** Good structure lets a worker stop at a sufficient abstract
  and load only the child documents required for the question.
- **Visual brief:** Show a reader with a finite woven basket walking a branching
  garden archive. At each fork, a concise sign lets the reader either stop or
  descend to one narrower bed. The basket holds a few complete cuttings; a huge
  untouched archive remains behind, making selective completeness preferable to
  indiscriminate collection.
- **Illuminated treatment:** Let branching stems double as the hierarchy, with
  small rubricated signboards and a border that becomes simpler toward the
  reader's basket.
- **Accessibility and pacing:** Horizontal 2:1 closing illustration. Alternative
  text should state the stop condition and finite context constraint. Place it
  before the authoring-discipline details so it frames the reason for the rules.

### 25. Inference tiers as matching ladders, not a hierarchy of workers

- **Target anchor:** `ch10-chapter-10-inference-tiers-reference`
- **Central theme:** Jobs receive capability tiers, worker kinds bind providers,
  role floors limit fallback, and provider failure changes routes without
  changing the work's meaning.
- **Visual brief:** Show four stepped terraces holding increasingly demanding
  plants, beside several ladders owned by different worker guilds. More than one
  ladder can reach the same terrace. A safety rail blocks some role-marked pots
  from descending below their floor, while a switchback path illustrates a
  permitted fallback. The plants, terraces, and ladders should prevent readers
  from mistaking worker kind for tier.
- **Illuminated treatment:** Use an almanac plate with calibrated terrace lines,
  small guild emblems, and seed-head marginalia. Keep the geometry orderly for a
  reference chapter.
- **Accessibility and pacing:** A wide 3:2 opener is sufficient for this short,
  table-heavy chapter; no section images are proposed. Alternative text should
  distinguish job tier, provider-bound worker kind, floor, and fallback. Avoid
  encoding rank only by vertical position; use shape and caption language too.

## Existing first-edition background

**Recommendation: repurpose it as title-page-only ornament, not as a flat
background.** The `art/title-garden.svg` scene introduced in pull requests 4
and 5 is quiet and technically well prepared, but its broad washes and scattered
edge plants do not explain the book's subject. At the low opacity required behind
title copy, most of its already sparse information disappears, which supports the
maintainer's judgment that it does not add much.

The production stage should remove the full-field backdrop treatment and test a
tighter reuse of its strongest material: crop or redraw the right-hand vine,
trellis, and lower bed as a title-page border or corner illumination around the
title block. That gives the existing work one restrained ceremonial role without
counting it as one of the 25 thematic images. If the cropped ornament still reads
as a faint generic garden rather than a deliberate frame, drop it instead of
preserving it for continuity. Do not repeat it behind chapter or section art.

The existing paper texture is a separate presentation layer, not an illustration.
Its future use can be judged with the anchored color scheme and contrast tests in
production. The existing garden-bed figure may remain as an interim asset, but the
new chapter 2 opener should replace its current burden of representing the whole
architecture chapter once the set is integrated.

## Coverage and integration notes

The proposal contains 25 images: ten chapter openers and fifteen section images.
Chapters 5, 6, and 10 are deliberately sparse because their tables and catalog
entries already create visual rhythm. Chapter 7 receives more section coverage
because its procedures are distinct spatial stories, while chapters 1, 3, 8,
and 9 use a few stronger conceptual pauses rather than an image for each heading.

The current generator prefixes GitHub-style heading slugs with the chapter key;
the anchors above copy its present output exactly. Integration should treat those
anchors as placement keys rather than infer placement from chapter numbers. A
future heading edit can change an anchor, so tests should verify that every
configured target exists and that no image silently falls back to a different
location.
